# FAQ

The first four questions are the ones Google showed under "People also ask" for UK searches on "saville swift analysis aptitude test" in November 2026. The rest come from Saville's own candidate FAQ topics and common candidate questions. Answers use Saville's documents where Saville has published something, and say so when it has not.

## What is a Swift aptitude test?

A short combination aptitude test from Saville Assessment that measures two or three aptitudes in one sitting, with one total score and sub-scores. Swift Analysis covers verbal, numerical and diagrammatic reasoning. Swift Comprehension covers verbal, numerical and error checking. See the [overview](test-overview.md).

## How can I pass my aptitude test?

There is no published pass mark, so think of it as doing as well as you can. Learn a method for each sub-test, practise under the clock, and keep moving in every block of questions. The [study plans](study-plans.md) set this out by day.

## What happens if you fail an aptitude test?

The employer decides what result it needs and what happens next. Saville's candidate page says retakes are decided by the organisation that invited you, so ask them. If you get another attempt, the [study plans](study-plans.md) show how to use the time before it.

## Can I take my aptitude test online?

Yes. Swift tests are designed for unsupervised online use from a personal link. Saville's practice page says Swift Analysis and Swift Comprehension cannot be taken on a mobile phone, so use a laptop or desktop.

## How long is the Saville Swift Analysis test?

18 minutes for 24 questions: three sub-tests of 8 questions and 6 minutes each, per Saville's technical summary.

## How long is Swift Comprehension?

9.5 minutes for 24 questions: Verbal Comprehension and Numerical Comprehension at 4 minutes each, and Error Checking at 1.5 minutes.

## Is it hard?

It is fast. Saville's technical summaries report average scores of about 61% correct on Swift Analysis and about 55% on Swift Comprehension, and say many candidates are expected to answer above half correctly. Not finishing comfortably is normal.

## Can I use a calculator?

For the numerical sub-tests, yes in practice: Saville's own practice tests list "Calculator, Pen, Paper" as recommended materials for Numerical Analysis and Numerical Comprehension, and pen and paper only for the other sub-tests. Check your real test's instructions.

## Is there negative marking?

Saville does not say on its live pages. Answer carefully and do not leave questions blank when time is nearly up.

## Does everyone get the same questions?

No. Saville describes Swift as linear-on-the-fly testing: a fixed-length test with items of equivalent difficulty drawn from a bank for each candidate.

## Who sees my results?

The organisation that invited you, according to Saville's candidate page. It decides who sees them and whether you get feedback.

## Can I take the test in another language?

Saville's candidate page says its assessments are available in over 40 languages. Ask the organisation that invited you what is available for yours.

## How do I ask for an adjustment?

Contact the organisation that invited you before you start, as early as possible. Saville's candidate page gives this advice.

## Is there an official practice test?

Yes. Saville's [practice tests page](https://www.savilleassessment.com/practice-tests) has free timed practice tests for Swift Analysis and Swift Comprehension, which you can repeat as many times as you like. Saville notes that the example questions may not match the difficulty of the real test.

## Where can I practise for free?

Saville's own practice tests, the 78 items in this repo, and the [free Saville Swift practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/saville-swift?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=saville-swift), which follows the Swift Analysis length and section order.
