# Timing and pacing

Swift tests are short on purpose. Pace decides more results than difficulty does, so it is worth planning.

## The budget

| Test | Sub-test | Questions | Time | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swift Analysis | Verbal Analysis | 8 | 6 min | 45 sec |
| | Numerical Analysis | 8 | 6 min | 45 sec |
| | Diagrammatic Analysis | 8 | 6 min | 45 sec |
| Swift Comprehension | Verbal Comprehension | 8 | 4 min | 30 sec |
| | Numerical Comprehension | 8 | 4 min | 30 sec |
| | Error Checking | 8 | 1.5 min | about 11 sec |

Source: Saville's technical summaries for the Analysis and Comprehension ranges (2022, archived copies, see [sources](sources.md)). The per-question column is our arithmetic.

## Testlets: the clock runs in blocks

Saville's technical summaries say the tests support an "individual time limit for groups of four questions (testlets)". Saville's own practice tests (checked 24 November 2026) show how that works:

| Sub-test | Practice test timing |
|---|---|
| Verbal, Numerical, Diagrammatic Analysis | "2 separately timed sections. Each section contains 4 questions which you have 3 minutes to answer." |
| Verbal, Numerical Comprehension | 2 separately timed sections of 4 questions, 2 minutes each |
| Error Checking | "8 questions which you have 1 minute and 30 seconds to answer" |

These are the practice-test timings. Your real test's instructions screen is the final word. The practical point: time cannot be borrowed from one block for another, so a stuck question costs you within its own block.

## A pacing plan

Our guidance, not a Saville rule:

1. **Check the clock after every second question.** In a block of four, you should be halfway through the time after two.
2. **Set a hard limit per question:** one minute in Analysis, 40 seconds in Comprehension, 15 seconds in Error Checking. When you hit it, choose your best answer and move on.
3. **Do the quick wins first in your head.** If a question is clearly long, give it a fast first read, pick the likeliest answer, and keep moving.
4. **Never leave a question blank** when the block's time is nearly up.

## Where time usually goes

- **Rereading the passage** for each verbal statement. Read once with purpose.
- **Exact calculation** when estimating would rule out four options.
- **Working out diagrammatic operators twice.** Write each rule down the first time.
- **Rechecking error-checking rows** that already matched.

## Practise against the clock

Do at least two timed runs.
