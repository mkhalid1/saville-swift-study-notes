# The Saville Swift tests explained

Saville Assessment's Swift tests are short "combination" aptitude tests: several sub-tests in one sitting, with one login and one overall score. They are known for their pace. The two you are most likely to meet are **Swift Analysis Aptitude**, used for graduate, professional and management roles, and **Swift Comprehension Aptitude**, used for administrative, customer service, operational and commercial roles. This page covers both.

## Quick facts

| | Swift Analysis Aptitude | Swift Comprehension Aptitude |
|---|---|---|
| Typical roles (Saville) | Graduates, management trainees, managers, professionals, directors | Administrative, customer service, operational and commercial roles |
| Sub-tests | Verbal Analysis, Numerical Analysis, Diagrammatic Analysis | Verbal Comprehension, Numerical Comprehension, Error Checking |
| Questions | 24 (8 per sub-test) | 24 (8 per sub-test) |
| Time | 18 minutes (6 per sub-test) | 9.5 minutes (4, 4 and 1.5) |
| Average per question | 45 seconds | About 24 seconds overall, 11 seconds in Error Checking |
| Device for Saville's practice test | Desktop only | Desktop only |

Sources: Saville's technical summaries for the [Analysis](https://web.archive.org/web/20250512142835/https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Analysis-Aptitude-Range-Technical-Summary.pdf) and [Comprehension](https://web.archive.org/web/20250902085101/https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Comprehension-Aptitude-Range-Technical-Summary.pdf) aptitude ranges (Willis Towers Watson, 2022), Saville's [aptitude tests page as archived in June 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260618044324/https://www.savilleassessment.com/aptitude-tests/), and Saville's live [practice tests page](https://www.savilleassessment.com/practice-tests), checked 24 November 2026.

**A note on these sources.** Saville redesigned its website in 2026. The technical summaries are no longer hosted there, and the live candidate pages say timings are shown inside your own assessment platform. The figures above come from Saville's own documents as archived copies. Your platform and instructions are the final word for your test. See [sources](sources.md).

## How the tests are built

Saville's technical summaries describe the same design for both ranges:

- **Testlets.** Each sub-test has its own time limit, and the technology supports "individual time limit for groups of four questions (testlets)". So the clock is short and runs in small blocks.
- **Linear-on-the-fly testing.** Each candidate gets a fixed-length test, but the questions are drawn from a large bank, with items of equivalent difficulty for different candidates. You and another candidate will not see the same questions.
- **IRT scoring.** Scores are based on item response theory, a statistical method that accounts for the difficulty of the questions you got.
- **Unsupervised online use.** You take it from a personal link. Saville also has supervised fixed-content versions for follow-up testing.

What this means for you: you cannot learn the questions, but you can learn the formats, a method for each, and the pace.

## Other Swift tests you may hear about

Saville's practice page and archived product page list other Swift combinations too:

| Test | What Saville says it covers |
|---|---|
| Swift Executive Aptitude | Verbal, numerical and abstract reasoning for senior roles, 18 minutes (28 questions per the technical summary) |
| Swift Analysis Verbal & Numerical | Verbal and numerical only, 24 minutes, 32 questions |
| Swift Comprehension Verbal & Numerical | Verbal and numerical only, 12 minutes, 24 questions |
| Swift Technical Aptitude | Spatial, mechanical and diagrammatic reasoning, about 10 minutes |
| Swift Apprentice Aptitude | Six aptitude areas in under 20 minutes |
| Swift Global Aptitude | Number, abstract reasoning and memory recall, 12 minutes |

This repo covers Swift Analysis and Swift Comprehension. See [which Swift test you have](which-swift-test.md) for how to tell them apart.

## The one habit that runs through every Swift test

Speed with discipline. Every sub-test rewards judging only what is in front of you, the passage, the table or the shapes, and doing it fast. The most common way to lose marks is spending a minute on one hard item and running out of time on three easier ones.

## Practise the real format

Read the section guides for your test, then practise under the clock.
