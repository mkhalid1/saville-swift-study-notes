# How Swift scoring works

Short version: you get one total score and a score for each sub-test. The total is the one Saville designed for decisions. Scores are compared with a norm group the employer picks, and there is no published pass mark.

## The total score comes first

Saville's archived aptitude page says recruiters receive "one total score and individual sub scores in a single easy-to-interpret report". Its technical summaries add that the greatest reliability is at the total score level, "which is designed to be the decision-making score", and that Saville would not recommend using the sub-test scores in isolation for decisions.

What this means for you: a weak sub-test does not sink you on its own. Your total across all three matters most. Do not abandon a sub-test you find hard. Every correct answer adds to the total.

## IRT scoring and different questions

Saville describes Swift as linear-on-the-fly testing with item response theory scoring. In plain terms:

- Each candidate gets a fixed number of questions, drawn from a large bank.
- Items are chosen to be of equivalent difficulty for different candidates.
- Scoring takes account of the difficulty of the specific items you answered.

So your result is not a simple count you can compare with a friend's. Saville does not publish the scoring formula, and we do not guess at it.

## Norm groups

Saville's technical summaries say international, regional and country norms are available, and its validity page says it keeps norms for groups such as senior managers and executives, professionals and managers, graduates and apprentices. The employer chooses the comparison group. The same performance can look different against graduates than against a general group.

## Most people do not get everything right

Saville's technical summaries report the average percentage of questions answered correctly in large samples:

| Test | Average correct | Sample |
|---|---|---|
| Swift Analysis Aptitude | 60.67% | 95,337 candidates |
| Swift Comprehension Aptitude | 54.69% | 35,949 candidates |
| Within Swift Comprehension: Verbal | 61.72% | |
| Within Swift Comprehension: Numerical | 52.48% | |
| Within Swift Comprehension: Error Checking | 49.86% | |

Saville says the design aim is "a positive candidate experience where many candidates answer above 50% of questions correctly". In other words, getting around four in ten wrong is normal. If the test feels hard and you do not finish everything, you are in good company.

## Pass marks and guessing

Saville does not publish a pass mark on its live public pages. Each employer decides what it needs and how much weight the test carries. Saville also does not state on its live pages whether wrong answers are penalised. Our advice: answer carefully, and when a testlet's time is nearly up, give your best answer rather than leave a question blank.

## Who sees your results

Saville's candidate page says the organisation that invited you sees your results and decides who in its team sees them, whether you get feedback and what happens next.

## Track your sub-test scores in practice

Even though the total matters most, practice scores by sub-test tell you where to improve.
