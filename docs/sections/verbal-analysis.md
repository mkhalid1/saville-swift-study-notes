# Verbal Analysis

**In one line:** you read a short workplace passage and decide whether a statement about it is true, false, or cannot be told from the passage.

Verbal Analysis is the first of the three sub-tests in **Swift Analysis Aptitude**. Saville's technical summary says it assesses the ability to "understand, interpret and evaluate written information", and gives it 8 questions in 6 minutes.

## What it measures

It looks like a reading test, and graduates who read a lot often expect it to be easy. The catch is that it asks you to switch off what you know. If a passage about a bus company says journey times fell after contactless payment came in, your common sense says contactless caused it. The passage does not say so, and the answer to "contactless caused the fall" is Cannot say.

So each statement gets one of three judgements. True if the passage says it, in any wording. False if the passage says something that rules it out. Cannot say if the passage leaves it open, however likely it sounds. Cannot say is the judgement people find hardest, because it feels like a non-answer.

## How the questions look

In Saville's own practice test (checked 24 November 2026), Verbal Analysis comes in two separately timed sections. Each section has one passage, or a pair of short sources, followed by four questions. The question types are mixed:

- **True, False or Cannot say.** Saville's wording for the third option is "Not possible to say if the statement is true or false on the basis of the passage".
- **Multiple choice with four text options,** such as which conclusion follows or what the passage implies.

The practice items in this repo drill the True, False, Cannot say judgement, because the same discipline also decides most multiple-choice items: only the passage counts.

| Option | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| **True** | The statement follows from the passage |
| **False** | The passage contradicts the statement |
| **Cannot say** | The passage does not give enough information to decide |

## A method that works

1. **Read the statement first.** Find its key claim: a number, a "all" or "only", a cause, a time.
2. **Find the sentence in the passage that deals with it.** Usually there is one. Sometimes there are two that combine.
3. **Ask: does that sentence prove it, rule it out, or neither?**
4. **If neither, it is Cannot say.** Do not fill the gap with what is likely.
5. **Watch the small words.** "Only", "all", "never", "most", "because" turn a true-sounding statement false or unknown.
6. **Do not reread the whole passage for every statement.** Keep a mental map of which sentence covers what.

## Traps that cost points

- **Using what you know.** If a passage says a hospital cut waiting times after hiring more nurses, you may be sure the nurses caused it. The passage only says one followed the other.
- **Reading a result as a reason.** "Applications rose after the advert campaign" does not tell you why they rose.
- **Missing "only", "all" and "every".** "The scheme is open to all staff" and "the scheme is open only to full-time staff" lead to opposite answers for a part-timer.
- **Calling silence False.** False needs the passage to rule the statement out. If the passage simply does not mention it, the answer is Cannot say.
- **Treating a claim as a fact.** "The director says profits will double" proves only that the director said it.

## Timing

Saville's technical summary gives 6 minutes for 8 questions, which works out to 45 seconds each. Saville's practice test splits this into "2 separately timed sections. Each section contains 4 questions which you have 3 minutes to answer." As our guidance: aim for 40 seconds a question, and never spend more than a minute on one.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Saville test. Choose True, False or Cannot say for each statement. Answer all the statements for a passage before opening the explanations.

### Passage 1

> A regional bus operator introduced contactless payment on all of its routes in March. Before the change, drivers spent an average of 20 seconds per passenger taking cash fares. The operator says average journey times on its busiest route fell by four minutes in the following quarter. Cash is still accepted, but the operator plans to review this next year. Passenger numbers across the network were unchanged over the same period.

**Item 1.** Contactless payment is available on every route the operator runs.

**Item 2.** The fall in journey times on the busiest route was caused only by faster fare payment.

**Item 3.** The operator has stopped accepting cash fares.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: True.** Contactless was introduced "on all of its routes". *Why not Cannot say:* "all of its routes" settles it.

**Item 2: Cannot say.** The passage reports that journey times fell and that cash fares used to take time, but it never says what caused the fall, let alone that faster payment was the only cause. *Why not True:* the link is plausible, but the passage does not state it.

**Item 3: False.** "Cash is still accepted." *Why not Cannot say:* the passage directly contradicts the statement.

</details>
