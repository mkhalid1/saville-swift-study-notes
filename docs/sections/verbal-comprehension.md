# Verbal Comprehension

**In one line:** you read a short, everyday work passage, such as a policy or a notice, and decide whether a statement about it is true, false, or cannot be told from the passage.

Verbal Comprehension is the first sub-test in **Swift Comprehension Aptitude**, the version Saville designed for administrative, customer service, operational and commercial roles. Saville's technical summary says it assesses the ability to "understand and interpret written information", with 8 questions in 4 minutes.

## What it measures

Think of the notices you read in a first week at a new job: the break policy, the holiday form, the opening hours on the door. That is the level of text here. The reasoning is simpler than in Verbal Analysis, but you get 30 seconds a question instead of 45, so the test is really about reading exactly what the notice says while the clock runs.

The marks usually go on conditions. "Breaks are 30 minutes for shifts of six hours or more" tells you about a seven-hour shift and a five-hour shift, and nothing at all about the staff canteen. Candidates who work in customer-facing jobs often answer from how their own workplace runs. Here, only the passage counts.

## How the questions look

In Saville's own practice test (checked 24 November 2026), Verbal Comprehension has two separately timed sections, each with one passage and four questions. The question types are mixed: True, False or "Not possible to say", multiple-choice questions with four options, and word-meaning questions, such as which word could replace one in the passage.

The practice items in this repo drill the True, False, Cannot say judgement, which is where most marks go:

| Option | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| **True** | The passage says it, or it follows directly |
| **False** | The passage says the opposite or rules it out |
| **Cannot say** | The passage does not cover it |

For word-meaning questions, pick the option that keeps the sentence's meaning in context, not the closest dictionary match.

## A method that works

1. **Read the statement first** and pick out its key detail: a time, an amount, a condition, a person.
2. **Scan the passage for that detail.** Passages here are short, so one quick scan is enough.
3. **Compare carefully.** Same condition? Same limit? Same person or place?
4. **If the passage is silent, choose Cannot say,** even if the statement sounds normal for a workplace.
5. **Move on.** At 30 seconds a question, you cannot reread passages twice.

## Traps that cost points

- **Stretching a rule to a new place.** If a hotel notice says "guests must show their key card at the pool", it tells you nothing about the restaurant.
- **Dropping a condition.** "Refunds within 30 days with a receipt" does not cover a refund on day 20 without one.
- **Swapping the person.** If the notice says the shift leader signs off overtime, a statement that the store manager does it is False, not Cannot say.
- **Filling in a number.** If a notice mentions a parking charge but not the amount, any statement about the amount is Cannot say.
- **Turning "part of" into "all of".** "Closed on Sunday mornings" is not "closed on Sundays".

## Timing

Saville gives 4 minutes for 8 questions: 30 seconds each. Saville's practice test splits this into "2 separately timed sections. Each section contains 4 questions which you have 2 minutes to answer." As our guidance: if a statement takes more than 40 seconds, pick the best option and move on.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Saville test. Choose True, False or Cannot say. Try to finish all three in 90 seconds.

### Passage 1

> Staff at Brookfield Garden Centre must wear their name badge at all times on the shop floor. Breaks are 30 minutes for shifts of six hours or more, and 15 minutes for shorter shifts. Holiday requests should be sent to the duty manager at least two weeks in advance. Requests for December are decided in order of arrival.

**Item 1.** A member of staff working a five-hour shift gets a 15-minute break.

**Item 2.** Name badges must be worn in the staff canteen.

**Item 3.** December holiday requests are decided by length of service.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: True.** Shifts shorter than six hours get a 15-minute break, and five hours is shorter than six.

**Item 2: Cannot say.** The badge rule covers the shop floor. The passage does not say whether it applies anywhere else. *Why not False:* the passage does not rule it out either.

**Item 3: False.** December requests are decided "in order of arrival", which rules out length of service.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | True | Cannot say | False |

- **3:** good. The challenge now is pace: 30 seconds each.
- **2:** check item 2. Cannot say is where most marks go.
- **0 to 1:** for each statement, find and point to the sentence that decides it. If there is none, the answer is Cannot say.
