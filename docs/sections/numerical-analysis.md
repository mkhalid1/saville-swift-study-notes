# Numerical Analysis

**In one line:** you read a table or chart and pick the right answer out of five, usually after a percentage, ratio or rate calculation.

Numerical Analysis is the second sub-test in **Swift Analysis Aptitude**. Saville's technical summary says it assesses the ability to "understand, interpret and evaluate numerical data", with 8 questions in 6 minutes.

## What it measures

The maths is school level: percentages, ratios, averages, rates. What the questions test is whether you can pick the right figures from a busy table, use the right method, and do it fast. Three skills matter most:

- **Selecting the data.** Which row, which column, which year, which unit.
- **Choosing the operation.** Change over the starting value. Part over the total. Weighting by size.
- **Working in two steps** without dropping a number.

## How the questions look

In Saville's own practice test (checked 24 November 2026), Numerical Analysis has two separately timed sections. Each section gives one data set, such as a table or a pair of charts, and four questions on it, with four answer options each. The practice items in this repo use five options, which makes them a little harder to guess.

Wrong options are often the result of a common slip, such as dividing by the wrong base. If your first answer matches an option, check you did not make one of those slips.

**Calculator:** Saville's practice test lists "Calculator, Pen, Paper" as recommended materials for this sub-test. Check your own instructions for the real test.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question first,** then find only the data it needs.
2. **Check the units and headings.** Per year or per month. Thousands or units. Percent or count.
3. **Write down the two or three figures you need.**
4. **Say the operation in words** before calculating: "change divided by the old value".
5. **Estimate,** then compare with the options. If only one option is near your estimate, take it.
6. **If your exact answer is not listed,** check the base and the unit first.

## Traps that cost points

- **Dividing by the new value.** A fall from 1,230 to 1,176 is 54 ÷ 1,230, not 54 ÷ 1,176.
- **Averaging percentages from groups of different sizes.** Weight each group by its size.
- **Comparing totals when the question asks about rates,** such as use per square metre.
- **Applying a percentage to the wrong figure,** for example to the old price when the question describes the new one.
- **Rounding too early.** Keep full figures until the last step.

## Timing

Saville gives 6 minutes for 8 questions: 45 seconds each. Saville's practice test splits this into "2 separately timed sections. Each section contains 4 questions which you have 3 minutes to answer." Numerical items take longer than verbal ones for most people, so aim for 40 seconds on the easy ones to buy time for the two-step ones.

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Saville test. Use a calculator and paper.

### Data set 1: Office electricity

| Office | Floor area (m²) | Electricity use 2024 (MWh) | Electricity use 2025 (MWh) | Price per MWh in 2025 (£) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leeds | 2,400 | 360 | 324 | 240 |
| Bristol | 1,800 | 300 | 312 | 250 |
| Glasgow | 3,000 | 420 | 399 | 230 |
| Cardiff | 1,200 | 150 | 141 | 260 |

**Item 1.** Which office cut its electricity use by the largest percentage from 2024 to 2025?

A. Leeds &nbsp; B. Bristol &nbsp; C. Glasgow &nbsp; D. Cardiff &nbsp; E. Leeds and Glasgow equally

**Item 2.** By what percentage did the four offices' combined electricity use fall from 2024 to 2025, to one decimal place?

A. 3.9% &nbsp; B. 4.4% &nbsp; C. 4.6% &nbsp; D. 5.4% &nbsp; E. 7.2%

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 and 2</summary>

**Item 1: A, Leeds.** Changes: Leeds 36 ÷ 360 = 10.0% down, Bristol up 4%, Glasgow 21 ÷ 420 = 5.0% down, Cardiff 9 ÷ 150 = 6.0% down. *Why not C:* Glasgow had the largest absolute use but a smaller percentage fall.

**Item 2: B, 4.4%.** 2024 total 1,230 MWh, 2025 total 1,176 MWh. Fall 54. 54 ÷ 1,230 = 4.39%, so 4.4%. *Why not C:* 4.6% divides by the 2025 total instead of the 2024 one.

</details>

### Data set 2: Customer contact survey

800 customers were asked their preferred way to contact the company. Each chose one channel.

| Age group | Respondents | Email | Phone | Chat | Post |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 to 34 | 300 | 40% | 10% | 45% | 5% |
| 35 to 54 | 280 | 50% | 25% | 20% | 5% |
| 55 and over | 220 | 35% | 45% | 5% | 15% |

**Item 3.** What percentage of all 800 respondents prefer phone, to one decimal place?

A. 23.3% &nbsp; B. 24.9% &nbsp; C. 26.7% &nbsp; D. 27.5% &nbsp; E. 80.0%

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation, item 3</summary>

**Item 3: B, 24.9%.** Phone: 10% of 300 = 30, 25% of 280 = 70, 45% of 220 = 99. Total 199. 199 ÷ 800 = 24.875%, so 24.9%. *Why not C:* 26.7% is the plain average of 10%, 25% and 45%, which ignores the different group sizes.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | A | B | B |

- **3:** accurate. Now work on speed: do the set again in 2.5 minutes.
- **2:** check items 2 and 3. They test the base and weighting slips that cost most people marks.
- **0 to 1:** slow down, say the operation out loud, and write the figures down before you calculate. Then rebuild speed.
