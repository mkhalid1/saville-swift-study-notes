# Numerical Comprehension

**In one line:** you read a simple table of work figures, such as prices, quantities or hours, and pick the right answer out of five, using everyday arithmetic.

Numerical Comprehension is the second sub-test in **Swift Comprehension Aptitude**. Saville's technical summary says it assesses the ability to "understand and interpret numerical data", with 8 questions in 4 minutes.

## What it measures

The maths is simpler than in Numerical Analysis: totals, unit costs, simple percentages, finding the largest or smallest. The challenge is doing it in 30 seconds a question without slips. The skills are:

- reading the right row and column quickly
- multiplying price by quantity, adding a column, finding a percentage of a total
- checking the unit, such as packs of 50 versus single items

## How the questions look

A small table, a question and five answer options. Typical data: an order list, a staff rota, a stock sheet, a delivery schedule. Saville does not publish its calculator policy on its live public pages, so check your invitation and platform, and have a calculator ready in case.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question,** then go straight to the row or column it needs.
2. **Check the unit in the header:** per kg, per pack, per hour.
3. **Estimate first.** Many options are far enough apart that a rough figure picks the answer.
4. **Calculate only when two options are close.**
5. **Move on quickly.** At 30 seconds each, an extra minute on one question costs you two others.

## Traps that cost points

- **Missing a pack size.** 12 packs of 50 cups is 600 cups, not 12.
- **Adding the wrong rows** when the question asks about only some of them.
- **Reading across the wrong line** in a rota. Keep a finger or the cursor on the name.
- **Treating a tie carelessly.** If two people look equal, add again before choosing.
- **Applying a percentage to the total** when it should apply to one item.

## Timing

Saville gives 4 minutes for 8 questions: 30 seconds each, in testlets of four with their own time limits. If the split is equal, that works out to about 2 minutes per testlet, which is our arithmetic, not a Saville figure.

## Practice items

These 10 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Saville test. Aim to finish all ten in 6 minutes.

### Table 1: A café's weekly supplies order

| Item | Unit | Price per unit (£) | Units ordered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milk | litre | 1.10 | 60 |
| Coffee beans | kg | 14.50 | 8 |
| Cups | pack of 50 | 4.20 | 12 |
| Sugar | kg | 1.30 | 5 |

**Item 1.** What is the cost of the milk in this order?

A. £60.00 &nbsp; B. £61.10 &nbsp; C. £66.00 &nbsp; D. £70.00 &nbsp; E. £72.60

**Item 2.** What is the total cost of the whole order?

A. £228.90 &nbsp; B. £232.40 &nbsp; C. £238.90 &nbsp; D. £244.50 &nbsp; E. £251.10

**Item 3.** How many individual cups are in the order?

A. 12 &nbsp; B. 50 &nbsp; C. 62 &nbsp; D. 500 &nbsp; E. 600

**Item 4.** The price of coffee beans rises by 10%. What would 8 kg cost at the new price?

A. £116.00 &nbsp; B. £124.40 &nbsp; C. £127.60 &nbsp; D. £130.50 &nbsp; E. £159.50

**Item 5.** Coffee beans make up roughly what share of the total order cost?

A. 34% &nbsp; B. 41% &nbsp; C. 46% &nbsp; D. 49% &nbsp; E. 52%

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C, £66.00.** 60 litres × £1.10 = £66.00.

**Item 2: C, £238.90.** Milk £66.00, coffee beans 8 × £14.50 = £116.00, cups 12 × £4.20 = £50.40, sugar 5 × £1.30 = £6.50. Total £238.90.

**Item 3: E, 600.** 12 packs of 50 cups is 600 cups. *Why not A:* 12 is the number of packs.

**Item 4: C, £127.60.** New price £14.50 × 1.10 = £15.95. 8 × £15.95 = £127.60. *Why not A:* £116.00 is the cost at the old price, before the rise.

**Item 5: D, 49%.** £116.00 ÷ £238.90 = 0.486, about 49%.

</details>

### Table 2: Hours worked in one week

| Name | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amir | 8 | 8 | 0 | 8 | 6 |
| Beth | 6 | 0 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Chen | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Dana | 0 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 |

**Item 6.** Who worked the most hours this week?

A. Amir &nbsp; B. Beth &nbsp; C. Chen &nbsp; D. Dana &nbsp; E. Amir and Beth equally

**Item 7.** How many hours were worked in total on Wednesday?

A. 12 &nbsp; B. 16 &nbsp; C. 20 &nbsp; D. 24 &nbsp; E. 28

**Item 8.** Chen is paid £11.50 an hour. What is Chen's pay for the week?

A. £184.00 &nbsp; B. £207.00 &nbsp; C. £230.00 &nbsp; D. £241.50 &nbsp; E. £253.00

**Item 9.** What percentage of all hours worked this week did Chen work, to one decimal place?

A. 16.7% &nbsp; B. 18.0% &nbsp; C. 20.0% &nbsp; D. 22.2% &nbsp; E. 25.0%

**Item 10.** On which day were the most hours worked in total?

A. Monday &nbsp; B. Tuesday &nbsp; C. Wednesday &nbsp; D. Thursday &nbsp; E. Friday

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 6 to 10</summary>

**Item 6: D, Dana.** Totals: Amir 30, Beth 30, Chen 20, Dana 31. *Why not E:* Amir and Beth are equal, but Dana is one hour ahead.

**Item 7: C, 20.** Wednesday: 0 + 8 + 4 + 8 = 20.

**Item 8: C, £230.00.** Chen worked 20 hours. 20 × £11.50 = £230.00.

**Item 9: B, 18.0%.** All hours: 30 + 30 + 20 + 31 = 111. 20 ÷ 111 = 0.180, so 18.0%. *Why not E:* 25% assumes the four people worked equal hours.

**Item 10: D, Thursday.** Daily totals: Monday 18, Tuesday 20, Wednesday 20, Thursday 27, Friday 26.

</details>
