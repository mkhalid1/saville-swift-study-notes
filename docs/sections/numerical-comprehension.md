# Numerical Comprehension

**In one line:** you read a simple table of work figures, such as prices, quantities or hours, and pick the right answer out of five, using everyday arithmetic.

Numerical Comprehension is the second sub-test in **Swift Comprehension Aptitude**. Saville's technical summary says it assesses the ability to "understand and interpret numerical data", with 8 questions in 4 minutes.

## What it measures

The maths is simpler than in Numerical Analysis: totals, unit costs, simple percentages, finding the largest or smallest. The challenge is doing it in 30 seconds a question without slips. The skills are:

- reading the right row and column quickly
- multiplying price by quantity, adding a column, finding a percentage of a total
- checking the unit, such as packs of 50 versus single items

## How the questions look

In Saville's own practice test (checked 24 November 2026), Numerical Comprehension has two separately timed sections, each with one table or chart and four questions. Most questions have five answer options, and some ask whether a statement about the data is True, False or "Not possible to say". Typical data: an order list, a staff rota, a stock sheet, a pie chart of sales.

**Calculator:** Saville's practice test lists "Calculator, Pen, Paper" as recommended materials for this sub-test. Check your own instructions for the real test.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question,** then go straight to the row or column it needs.
2. **Check the unit in the header:** per kg, per pack, per hour.
3. **Estimate first.** Many options are far enough apart that a rough figure picks the answer.
4. **Calculate only when two options are close.**
5. **Move on quickly.** At 30 seconds each, an extra minute on one question costs you two others.

## Traps that cost points

- **Missing a pack size.** 12 packs of 50 cups is 600 cups, not 12.
- **Adding the wrong rows** when the question asks about only some of them.
- **Reading across the wrong line** in a rota. Keep a finger or the cursor on the name.
- **Treating a tie carelessly.** If two people look equal, add again before choosing.
- **Applying a percentage to the total** when it should apply to one item.

## Timing

Saville gives 4 minutes for 8 questions: 30 seconds each, Saville's practice test splits this into "2 separately timed sections. Each section contains 4 questions which you have 2 minutes to answer."

## Practice items

These 3 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any Saville test. Aim to finish all three in 2 minutes.

### Table 1: A café's weekly supplies order

| Item | Unit | Price per unit (£) | Units ordered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milk | litre | 1.10 | 60 |
| Coffee beans | kg | 14.50 | 8 |
| Cups | pack of 50 | 4.20 | 12 |
| Sugar | kg | 1.30 | 5 |

**Item 1.** How many individual cups are in the order?

A. 12 &nbsp; B. 50 &nbsp; C. 62 &nbsp; D. 500 &nbsp; E. 600

**Item 2.** The price of coffee beans rises by 10%. What would 8 kg cost at the new price?

A. £116.00 &nbsp; B. £124.40 &nbsp; C. £127.60 &nbsp; D. £130.50 &nbsp; E. £159.50

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 and 2</summary>

**Item 1: E, 600.** 12 packs of 50 cups is 600 cups. *Why not A:* 12 is the number of packs.

**Item 2: C, £127.60.** New price £14.50 × 1.10 = £15.95. 8 × £15.95 = £127.60. *Why not A:* £116.00 is the cost at the old price, before the rise.

</details>

### Table 2: Hours worked in one week

| Name | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amir | 8 | 8 | 0 | 8 | 6 |
| Beth | 6 | 0 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Chen | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Dana | 0 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 |

**Item 3.** What percentage of all hours worked this week did Chen work, to one decimal place?

A. 16.7% &nbsp; B. 18.0% &nbsp; C. 20.0% &nbsp; D. 22.2% &nbsp; E. 25.0%

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation, item 3</summary>

**Item 3: B, 18.0%.** Chen worked 20 hours. All hours: 30 + 30 + 20 + 31 = 111. 20 ÷ 111 = 0.180, so 18.0%. *Why not E:* 25% assumes the four people worked equal hours.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | E | C | B |

- **3:** accurate. Now aim for 30 seconds each.
- **2:** look for unit slips (item 1) and adding slips (item 3).
- **0 to 1:** write each figure down before calculating. Speed comes after accuracy.
